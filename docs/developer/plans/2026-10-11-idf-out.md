## Problem

*Convert2IDF* writes a file that holds "the minimum to run once weather and outputs are chosen" (D-112, ADR-010), and EnergyPlus is never run. The next step on the roadmap is the first EnergyPlus run. The owner wants the exported IDF ready to simulate with the settings a study needs, and a way to run EnergyPlus end to end from the repository.

### What a first run showed (2026-10-11, before this note)

EnergyPlus 26.2.0 (`D:\EnergyPlusV26-2-0`, build `4bd7a1f26f`) is installed on the development machine with five TMY3 weather files. All 1073 IDFs the export tests write (`BEMGEN_IDF_OUT`) were run as they are with the Chicago O'Hare TMY3 file:

- **All 1073 completed** (exit code 0). The same 1073 pass `scripts/idd-check` against the 26.2.0 IDD with 0 problems.
- **Warnings**, by kind:
  1. `Version: in IDF="25.2" not the same as expected="26.2"`: every file.
  2. `Output:Meter: invalid Key Name="NATURALGAS:FACILITY"`: 1015 files, every building without gas equipment.
  3. `Entered Zone Floor Area(s) differ more than 5% from the sum of the Space Floor Area(s)`: 491 files, every *Single Zone Building* and *Single Zone per Floor Type* building. This is by design (ADR-010: a zone spanning storeys states its floor area). The run confirms that EnergyPlus uses the stated area. For example, the linear plan under *Perimeter Core* and *Single Zone Building* states 1728 m², EnergyPlus reports 1728 m², and lighting is 4.667 W/m² × 1728 m² = 8064 W. This settles the first of ADR-010's items "to verify on the first EnergyPlus run".
  4. `... Flow Rate per Exterior Surface Area, but Exterior Surface Area = 0. 0 Infiltration will result.`: 12 occurrences in 4 files, the core zones of *Perimeter Core* under the two area bases of infiltration. BEMGen's own design flow for those zones is 0 too (`ZoneInfiltration.DesignFlow`), so the result is right, but the warning is noise.
- **One severe error, 3 files**: `L1/CONDITIONED-1/F14 is a casting surface and is non-convex. Shadowing values may be inaccurate`. This is the stair-bay bar under *Conditioned Merge* with *Join Pieces* and the upper storey moved so that it overhangs. The exposed floor of the overhang is non-convex and shades the roof below. ADR-010's "`FullExterior` does not need convex zones" holds for zones, not for casting surfaces.
- The window construction named like its glazing material (ADR-010's second item) runs without a message.

So the files already run. "Simulation-ready" means the settings D-112 left open, a clean error file, and the runner.

### The floor aggregators against the reference (2026-10-11, before this note)

The owner asked for one case per floor aggregator, with the results inspected to confirm the aggregators are implemented correctly.

**Setup.** The IDFs of four plans (the canonical linear plan with multipliers 1, 2, 1; the office plate 1, 3, 1; the enclosed court 1, 3, 1; the stepped band's three storeys) were taken under *No Simplification* through each of the four floor aggregators, with both heat-transfer settings. All 32 runs completed with no severe error.

**Conservation holds through EnergyPlus.** In every plan, under every aggregator and both settings:

- the zones' floor area and volume times multiplier, as EnergyPlus reads them, equal the reference's (1728, 6300, 1404, and 8640 m²);
- the hourly internal loads (lighting, equipment, hot water) equal the reference's at every hour, to within 6e-11 W.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Linear | +0.5 % / +0.6 % / +0.3 % | +25.7 % / +5.4 % / +8.3 % | +26.1 % / +4.8 % / +8.2 % |

*Stack* with both options off differs from the reference by −7.2 % heating (linear) and −9.6 % (court). Interzone heat transfer matters, which supports the owner's choice of reference.

**Reading.** Each difference has a designed cause, so the four aggregators behave as ADR-009 and ADR-013 specify:

1. **The single-zone aggregators condition every zone they merge.** The linear plan's unconditioned stair (288 m²) is conditioned under *Single Zone per Floor Type* and *Single Zone Building*: 1440 m² conditioned becomes 1728 m². Heating rises by about a quarter, while the EUI per conditioned area moves less (25.8 → 27.0 kWh/m²). This is the rule the research brief §8 gives for Z3 across a floor, applied across storeys. The office plate, all conditioned, shows the plain geometry effect of one zone: within 2 %. It is why the experiment outputs report EUI per conditioned and per total area.
2. **Under *Stacked Floor Zone Multiplier* every slab between storeys is adiabatic,** whatever *EnableFloorHeatTransfer* says (ADR-013 option a, D-075). The stepped band has no multiplier (every storey differs, so each is split off as × 1, D-046). Its *Stacked Floor Zone Multiplier* file differs from *Stack* only in its 28 floor and ceiling pieces between storeys (and its provenance line): `Adiabatic` in place of paired. That gives +0.8 % cooling with the options on, and results identical to *Stack* with them off. This is as designed, though not obvious when every multiplier is 1 (finding F1 below).
3. **Unmodelled storeys cast no shadow.** Under *Stacked Floor Zone Multiplier* the court's storeys L1 and L3 are represented by L2 × 3 and not modelled, so the court is empty at their heights. In annual window-transmitted solar (`Enclosure Windows Total Transmitted Solar Radiation Energy`):
   - L0 receives 44.4 MWh in place of 39.5;
   - L2 receives 45.9 MWh in place of the 42.6 MWh mean of L1 to L3;
   - in total the building receives 229.4 MWh in place of 214.6 MWh (+6.9 %).

   This gives the +4.7 % cooling and −2.9 % heating. ADR-009 names this effect as part of the geometry effect under study and claims no engine behaviour. The run measures it: it is small where nothing shades (linear, office plate) and large in a court (finding F2 below).

**Findings for the owner (not changed by this stage).** They are geometry or research rules (GLOBAL.md scientific rule 7):

- **F1.** *Stacked Floor Zone Multiplier* could pair the slabs between two modelled × 1 storeys that are adjacent in the assembly, as *Stack* does, so that the method differs from *Stack* only where it multiplies.
- **F2.** The storeys a multiplied storey stands for could cast shadows: shading surfaces (`Shading:Building:Detailed`) at their walls, written by the converters, would restore the court's self-shading without modelling the zones.

  **A trial of F2** (2026-10-11, by hand, not committed). The 44 outdoor walls of the court's representative storey L2 were copied 3 m down and 3 m up, to where L1 and L3 stand, as 88 opaque `Shading:Building:Detailed` surfaces. The results against *Stack* (both options on):

  | Case | Window solar | Heating | Cooling |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | *Stack* (reference) | 214.6 MWh | 206.7 MWh | 276.3 MWh |
  | *Stacked Floor Zone Multiplier*, empty gaps | +6.9 % | −2.9 % | +4.7 % |
  | the same with the 88 shading surfaces | +0.1 % | +0.1 % | +0.9 % |

  L0's and L2's window solar return exactly to their *Stack* values (39.5 and 42.7 MWh). The 0.9 % cooling left over is the multiplier itself and the adiabatic slabs (F1).

**The owner on F1 and F2 (2026-10-11):** F1 stays a finding. F2 is taken up in this stage: unmodelled storeys cast shadows (Part G).

## Part B: weather and site

1. **`EpwHeader`** (`Lod.Export.Idf`, a plain parser over text, so tests need no file): it reads the `LOCATION` line (city, state or province, country, source, WMO number, latitude, longitude, time zone, elevation) and the `GROUND TEMPERATURES` line (depths and their twelve monthly values). It also reads the `DATA PERIODS` line to check that the data are hourly (one record per hour). Anything malformed is the error `InvalidWeatherFile`, which names the line and the field. Only the eight header lines are read, never the hourly records.
2. **`IdfOptions.Weather`** (`IdfWeather?`: the header, the file name, and the SHA-256 of the file's bytes, computed by `Convert2Idf` when it reads the path). With a weather file, the IDF holds `Site:Location` from the `LOCATION` line, named `<city> <state> <country>`, and the header records the file name, its checksum, and the location (GLOBAL.md scientific rule 2). Without one, there is no `Site:Location`, EnergyPlus takes the location from the weather file it runs with, and the info `NoWeatherFile` says so. Provenance gains the line `WEATHER:`.
3. **The run period stays the building's calendar** (ADR-018): a non-leap year and its weekday, with the weather file's holidays and daylight saving off, so that the hourly schedules stay on their days. EnergyPlus reads the weather file's hours in order whatever years it records, as TMY files mix years. No sizing: ideal loads have no capacity limit (D-112), so `SimulationControl` keeps sizing off and no design days are written. A future sizing run would read the `.ddy` beside the `.epw`.
4. **The ground model: a choice for the owner** (decision 1). The owner judged ISO 13370:2017 more than needed (2026-10-11) and asked for the established ways. EnergyPlus offers four, two of which suit this study:

   | Method | How it works in EnergyPlus | Inputs | Same at every level of detail? |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | **F-factor (recommended)** | `Construction:FfactorGroundFloor` per ground surface (F-factor, area, exposed perimeter) with the boundary `GroundFCfactorMethod`. EnergyPlus builds an equivalent construction whose conductance is F × exposed perimeter, against `Site:GroundTemperature:FCfactorMethod`: the weather file's monthly ground temperatures at 0.5 m, written explicitly from the `.epw` header. This is the method of ASHRAE 90.1 Appendix G and of the DOE prototypes: the `ASHRAE901_*_STD2019` example files shipped with EnergyPlus 26.2 use `Construction:FfactorGroundFloor`. | an F-factor per envelope (W/m·K); each surface's exposed perimeter | **The total, yes:** Σ F·P is F times the footprint's exposed perimeter, which every configuration keeps. The split between zones follows each zone's exposed perimeter: perimeter zones lose more, a core zone nothing. That is a real geometry effect, which the study is meant to measure. |
   | Weather-derived `Site:GroundTemperature:BuildingSurface`, by the slab preprocessor | `GroundHeatTransfer:Slab` (EnergyPlus's auxiliary `Slab` program) simulates an equivalent square slab of the footprint's area-to-perimeter ratio for a weather file and gives twelve monthly temperatures under the building. The envelope's ground-floor construction is kept. | the footprint's area and perimeter; soil and slab properties; a preprocessor run per footprint and weather | yes, one set per building |
   | "Indoor minus 2 °C" | The EnergyPlus documentation's rule of thumb for large slabs: the ground under a conditioned building is about 2 °C below the mean indoor temperature, for example 18 °C under 20 °C. | an indoor temperature | yes, but not weather-derived, so it ignores the climate (research brief §13) |
   | Fixed 18 °C (today) | What EnergyPlus assumes without input | none | yes, not weather-derived |
   | `Foundation:Kiva` (for comparison) | A two-dimensional numerical ground model per foundation | slab and soil details | no: per surface, and costly |

   **A trial** (2026-10-11, by hand, not committed). The linear plan's ground floors were rewritten as F-factor floors with F = 1.264 W/m·K (ASHRAE 90.1's unheated, uninsulated slab, F-0.73, as a placeholder), each piece's exposed perimeter measured on the footprint, and the default 0.5 m weather temperatures, then run with the Chicago weather file:
   - The ground pieces sum to the footprint's area and perimeter (432 m², 84 m) in every configuration.
   - The *Perimeter Core* core floor, with no exposed perimeter, is accepted without a warning.
   - Against the fixed 18 °C, heating rises by 10 to 11 % and cooling by about 5 % (the 0.5 m temperatures run from −3 °C to 23 °C in Chicago).

   **The owner's decision (2026-10-11): indoor minus 2 °C.** The F-factor method was preferred if BEMGen has what it needs. It has the weather file's 0.5 m temperatures, but not an F-factor. An F-factor depends on how the slab edge is insulated (ASHRAE 90.1's tables) and cannot be derived from the envelope's layers alone, so the method waits for sourced envelope values (D-023). The rule as built:
   - The writer writes `Site:GroundTemperature:BuildingSurface` with twelve monthly values T_g,m = T_in,m − 2 °C. The envelope's ground-floor construction is kept, so both converters describe the ground floor alike.
   - T_in,m is the mean, over the hours of month m, of the building's hourly setpoint, weighted by floor area times multiplier over the conditioned zones. A zone's hourly setpoint is the midpoint of its heating and cooling setpoints where it has both, and the one it has otherwise.
   - The value is the same at every level of detail. Merges weight setpoints by floor area over the conditioned sources (ADR-005, ADR-018), and a merge that conditions an unconditioned zone gives it the merged setpoint. So the area-weighted mean over conditioned zones is the same before and after any merge. This is exact when every conditioned zone has the same sides (both, heating only, or cooling only). A merge of zones with different sides is reported in the build log if a test meets one.
   - A building without a conditioned zone uses T_in = 20 °C, and so 18 °C, the value used before, with the info `GroundTemperatureDefault`.
   - The values and the rule are recorded in the header and in provenance. The tests check the twelve values, their invariance across every simplifier and aggregator of the linear plan, and the case without a conditioned zone.

## Part C: the research outputs

The outputs follow [Experiment outputs](../research/experiment-outputs.md): the owner's experiment output specification (2026-10-11), integrated with BEMGen and EnergyPlus 26.2. That page fixes what each case must yield, and from which EnergyPlus output each quantity comes. The first run showed that `Heating:EnergyTransfer` and `Cooling:EnergyTransfer` hold only the zones' sensible heat. The ideal loads' total heating and cooling go to `Heating:DistrictHeatingWater` and `Cooling:DistrictCooling`. `DistrictHeatingWater:Facility` also holds hot water (`WaterSystems:DistrictHeatingWater`), so heating comes from the end-use meter. They are written after the HVAC objects, in this order:

1. `Output:VariableDictionary,IDF;` (so a reader can see what is available) and `Output:SQLite,SimpleAndTabular;`.
2. `OutputControl:Table:Style,CommaAndHTML;` and `Output:Table:SummaryReports,AllSummary;`. AllSummary holds the *Building Area* table (total and net conditioned area, the normaliser of the experiment outputs; confirmed in a 26.2 run), the zone summary, and the hours a setpoint is not met.
3. `Output:Variable` with key `*` at `Hourly`:
   - `Zone Mean Air Temperature`, a raw output;
   - as diagnostics, when some zone is conditioned: `Zone Ideal Loads Zone Sensible Heating Energy`, `Zone Ideal Loads Zone Sensible Cooling Energy`, `Zone Ideal Loads Zone Total Heating Energy`, and `Zone Ideal Loads Zone Total Cooling Energy`.
4. `Output:Meter` at `Hourly` (was monthly), **only for what the building uses**:
   - `Heating:DistrictHeatingWater`, `Cooling:DistrictCooling`, `Heating:EnergyTransfer`, `Cooling:EnergyTransfer`, `DistrictHeatingWater:Facility`, and `DistrictCooling:Facility`, with any conditioned zone;
   - `Electricity:Facility`, with any lights or electric equipment;
   - `InteriorLights:Electricity`, with lights;
   - `InteriorEquipment:Electricity`, with electric equipment;
   - `NaturalGas:Facility` and `InteriorEquipment:NaturalGas`, with gas equipment;
   - `WaterSystems:DistrictHeatingWater` and `DistrictHeatingWater:Facility`, with hot water.

   This removes warning 2. The meter names were confirmed in a 26.2 run's `.mtd`.

The frequency, variables, and meters live in `IdfDefaults` as one named set (`IdfOutputs.Research`), so that a later selectable set needs no new mechanism. The set is not a component input in this stage. The IDF header gains the identifying lines of the case record (experiment outputs §7): the building ID, the zoning configuration ID, and the weather file and its checksum. The runner reads them back.

## Part D: a clean error file

1. **Infiltration where the basis has no area.** A zone whose infiltration basis is per exterior surface area or per exterior wall area (D-124) and which has none of that area gets no `ZoneInfiltration:DesignFlowRate` object. Its design flow is 0 in BEMGen and in EnergyPlus alike, so nothing is lost. The info `InfiltrationNotWritten` names the zones, and the infiltration checks count one object per zone with a non-zero design flow. This removes warning 4.
2. **Non-convex outdoor surfaces are split into convex pieces** (the owner, 2026-10-11: option b).
   - **Why.** EnergyPlus projects every shading surface onto every receiving surface and clips the polygons, which is reliable only when the shading surface is convex. A non-convex caster is a severe error ("shadowing values may be inaccurate"), and a non-convex receiver a warning.
   - **What.** Every surface written `Outdoors` that is not convex is split deterministically into convex pieces with the same total area, reported with the info `SurfaceSplit`, as floors with holes are split now: roofs (L, U, court, and hub footprints), exposed floors, any wall that is not convex, and the shading surfaces of Part G. Surfaces facing the ground, adiabatic surfaces, and surfaces between zones take no part in shading and stay whole. Windows are rectangles and are not affected.
   - **The cost.** More surfaces for every non-rectangular footprint, and a longer shading calculation. Most IDF snapshots change in their roof surfaces. The areas, and with them every invariant and the infiltration, are unchanged.
3. **Zone floor area (warning 3) stays.** It is the documented behaviour of zones spanning storeys and confirms the stated area is used. The runner lists it as an expected warning (Part E), with the reason.

## Part E: the EnergyPlus runner (C#)

**Rule change.** D-016 keeps simulation out of the pipeline, and D-112 keeps EnergyPlus out of the tests. D-130 refines D-016 and supersedes D-112's "not run":

- The pipeline still ends at the converters. A run is not a pipeline step: it takes a written IDF file and a weather file, never an `IGeneratedBuilding`, and changes no pipeline object.
- A new library, `Lod.Simulation`, runs an installed EnergyPlus and reads what the run reports.
- Tests may run EnergyPlus only when an installation is named (opt-in, below). Plain `dotnet test` still needs no EnergyPlus.

GLOBAL.md gains an architecture rule to that effect (proposed text: "7. Simulation is not a pipeline step. An EnergyPlus run, through `Lod.Simulation`, takes a written IDF and a weather file and changes no pipeline object; plain `dotnet test` never requires EnergyPlus."). AGENTS.md's boundary table gains a row.

1. **`Lod.Simulation`** (`netstandard2.0`, so the deferred Grasshopper component can use it). It depends on `Lod.Core` (for `Result` and `Diagnostic`) and must not depend on RhinoCommon, Grasshopper, or `Lod.Export`.
   - `EnergyPlusInstallation.Locate(folder)`: checks `energyplus.exe` and `Energy+.idd`, and reads the version from the IDD's first line (`!IDD_Version 26.2.0`) without starting a process.
   - `EnergyPlusRunRequest(IdfPath, WeatherPath, OutputFolder, Timeout)`.
   - `IProcessRunner`, injected (AGENTS.md: dependency injection for external services), with the default `SystemProcessRunner`. It takes a `CancellationToken`, so that a component can cancel later.
   - `EnergyPlusRunner.Run(request, token)` → `Result<EnergyPlusRun>`: exit code, elapsed time, output folder, and the parsed reports. It never writes to the console (GLOBAL.md quality rule 7). A missing installation, IDF, or weather file, a timeout, or an output folder that exists and is not empty is a structured error.
   - `EnergyPlusErrorReport.Parse(text)`: each warning, severe, and fatal with its continuation lines, the summary counts, and whether the run completed. `EnergyPlusZoneReport.Parse(eio)`: the `Zone Information` lines (name, multiplier, floor area, volume), so a test can compare EnergyPlus's zones with the building's.
   - A version mismatch between the IDF and the installation is the warning `EnergyPlusVersionMismatch`.
   - **Raw outputs** (experiment outputs §8), read from text files with the BCL only (no SQLite library, so the deferred component loads no native code):
     - `EsoReader` reads `eplusout.mtr` and `eplusout.eso` (data dictionary, then hourly records) into named hourly series of 8760 values;
     - `EnergyPlusZoneReport` reads `eplusout.eio`;
     - `TabularReport` reads the *Building Area* table of `eplustbl.csv`;
     - the runtime comes from `eplusout.err`.

     Together they make one `SimulationResult`: the hourly heating, cooling, and total load, the end-use meters, zone temperatures with zone areas and multipliers, total and conditioned floor area, the runtime and its conditions, and the case record. A meter that is not written reads as zero every hour, and a series that is not 8760 hours long is an error.
   - **Metrics** (experiment outputs §2 to §5; the owner placed them here): `ExperimentMetrics`, pure functions over `SimulationResult`, each with its zero guard tested:
     - the annual sums and EUI per conditioned and per total floor area;
     - the peaks and type 7 percentiles;
     - the hours above the reference's thresholds;
     - CV(RMSE), quantile NRMSE, and Kolmogorov–Smirnov;
     - the area-weighted temperature mean and spread, over all zones and over conditioned zones.

     The fixtures are small series with values worked by hand. The percentiles are also checked against NumPy's type 7 results, written into the test as literals.
2. **The check tool** `scripts/eplus-check/` is a C# console project (`net8.0`, in `BEMGen.sln` so that the verify gate builds it) over `Lod.Simulation`. It replaces nothing.
   - `dotnet run --project scripts/eplus-check -c Release -- --energyplus <folder> --weather <file.epw> <file.idf | folder>...` runs every file, in parallel, into a results folder outside the repository.
   - It prints one line per file. It exits with 1 on any fatal, any severe, a run that did not complete, or a warning that is not on its list of expected warnings. That list starts with the zone floor area of zones spanning storeys (Part D.3), and each entry carries its reason.
   - Its README records the last run, as `idd-check`'s does.
3. **Tests** `tests/Lod.Simulation.Tests` (`net8.0`, in `verify.ps1` like the others):
   - Always: the parsers on short fixture texts written for the tests, the installation check on a temporary folder, and the runner with a fake `IProcessRunner` (arguments, timeout, cancellation, a missing file, a failed run).
   - **End to end, opt-in.** `[EnergyPlusFact]` (an `xunit` `FactAttribute` that sets `Skip` when `BEMGEN_ENERGYPLUS` does not name an installation; no new package) generates buildings, converts them with the installation's own Chicago weather file, runs them, and asserts:
     - the run completed, with 0 severe errors and only expected warnings;
     - the zones of the `.eio` equal the building's zones in name, multiplier, floor area, and volume;
     - the `.sql` and the tables were written;
     - the `SimulationResult` is complete (8760 hours in each series), and its end uses sum to its total load hour by hour;
     - the conditioned floor area and the multiplied zone areas equal the building's;

## Part G: unmodelled storeys cast shadows

**The owner's decision (2026-10-11).** Self-shading is enabled for the floor-multiplier approach. This revises the reading of finding F2 above: the storeys a multiplied storey stands for are not modelled as zones, but they cast shadows. It supersedes in part ADR-009's acceptance of the empty gaps as part of the geometry effect under study, and the research brief §11 (V4) changes with it.

1. **What casts.** Under *Stacked Floor Zone Multiplier*, every storey of the assembled stack that is represented but not modelled (`StackedFloorZoneMultiplier.UnmodelledStoreys`, which the preview already draws grey) contributes its outdoor walls, at their true elevations, as opaque shading surfaces. Windows are part of the opaque surface: sun entering a window of an unmodelled storey does not reach another zone.
   - An unmodelled storey never has an exposed floor or a roof, because such storeys are split off as × 1 (D-046). So walls are enough, and the shading surfaces of a group are exactly the faces that the empty gaps leave absent.
   - The other aggregators model every storey and add no shading surface.
2. **Where it is made: at converter level** (the owner, 2026-10-11). Both *Convert2IDF* and *Convert2BEM* write the shading; the building does not carry it, so no pipeline type changes.
   - **Shared rule.** Both converters call one shared rule in `Lod.Core.Conversion`, as they share `ConstructionRoles` and `HeatTransferOptions.Exported`. `UnmodelledStoreyShading.Of(building)` assembles the stack from the building's floor entries (`IGeneratedBuilding.Sources`, `Storeys.Assemble`). It takes every storey of the stack that no zone of the building covers in height, and returns that storey's outdoor walls at their true elevation, with the IDs `S{k}/<wall ID>`.
   - **No special cases.** The rule names no aggregator and reads no provenance text. Under *Stack*, *Single Zone per Floor Type*, and *Single Zone Building* every storey is covered, so it returns nothing. Under *Stacked Floor Zone Multiplier* it returns the unmodelled storeys.
   - **Outputs.** *Convert2IDF* writes them as `Shading:Building:Detailed`. *Convert2BEM* gains an appended output, *Shading* (surfaces), for ClimateStudio. Both record the count and area in provenance.
3. **Checks.**
   - The shared rule's tests check that the shading area per unmodelled storey equals that storey's outdoor wall area in the assembly, and that no shading appears under the other aggregators. Validation does not change, because shading is not part of the building.
   - The shading surfaces take no part in any zone invariant.
   - The IDF parse-back checks that every shading surface is planar, simple, and convex after Part D.2.
4. **Evidence.** The F2 trial above: the court's window solar returns to within 0.1 % of *Stack*, heating to +0.1 %, and cooling to +0.9 %. The end-to-end floor-aggregator set repeats the comparison with the shading the code writes.
5. **Records.** D-130 records the rule change. ADR-013 gains a section, and ADR-009 is marked superseded in part. The research brief §11 V4 and the experiment outputs are updated in the same change (GLOBAL.md scientific rule 7).
