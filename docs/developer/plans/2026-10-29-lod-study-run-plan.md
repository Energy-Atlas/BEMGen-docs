## 1. Design in one paragraph

Each test case is one DOE/PNNL prototype building, generated by BEMGen's DOE generator at the prototype's own
dimensions. Its zones take the prototype's own atlas 2.0.0 programs, with deterministic ruleset schedules. The
envelope is the atlas 90.1 construction set for that vintage and climate zone, and the weather file is the
prototype's representative city for the zone. The reference site is the prototype's full zoning. The test sites
are the zoning simplifications, while geometry, programs, envelope and weather stay fixed (D-133). Using the
prototypes keeps geometry, programs and constructions from one consistent source, which is what the atlas
records. The 17 synthetic typologies are a later secondary study.

## 3. Changes needed before the scripts can run

All six were built on `claude/lod-study-scripts` (D-185, scripts/lod-study (`scripts/lod-study/README.md`)).

1. **Climate zone and context reach the program import.** `scripts/TestSuiteGenerator/Inputs/ProgramFiles.cs:138-147`
   should pass `ClimateZone` (from the test case's weather) and `Context`, so that climate-dependent values stop
   falling back with `ClimateZoneNotGiven`. It should also use the JSON `fenestration` when the spec says `wwr: program`.
2. **The DOE generators enter the suite registry** (`Registry/GeneratorRegistry.cs`), with a `doe:` spec block:
   prototype, vintage and orientation, plus the parameters from the DOE record. Zones take atlas program IDs through
   `DoeProgramMap` and the integration branch's `DoeReferenceProgramSelection`.
3. **A pinned weather set.** A script fetches the six EPWs, checks their SHA-256 and writes
   `D:/bemgen-weather/registry.json`. The pins are committed in the repo.
4. **Study specs and a driver.** `research/lod-study/specs/{smoke,pilot,main,ht,timing}.yaml` and
   `scripts/lod-study/run.ps1`, which runs generate → run → merge → report with `--resume`.
5. **Dedup and report script.** It reads the central DuckDB, applies the dedup rule from decision 8, and writes
   the §10 metric tables per prototype, climate and zoning.
6. **Re-made inputs.** The mock-1 and pilot specs use program JSON 2.0.0 and `discard_ep_results`, which v1.5.0
   refuses, so none of them is reused.

The integration branch's research gates run as preflight: floor research eligibility, exact composition, and
program signature, which together check that a zoning keeps programs and controls equal. The floor gate runs in the
generator's `report` mode, which records each site's verdict without blocking it: in `require` mode it blocks every zoning
that merges zones of different programs, 16 of the medium office's 24 (§7). The exact-composition screen is not run by the
generator.

## 8. The causes of the R1 warnings (D-187)

The owner chose "find the cause first" (2026-10-29). Scratch runs in `D:\bgdiag`, EnergyPlus 26.2.0.

**`PsyPsatFnTemp` is EnergyPlus's outdoor wet-bulb calculation, not a BEMGen object.** On the New York reference the warning
stays, at the same time step and with the same input (03/12 11:50, −170.82 °C), with the ideal loads' dehumidification control
off, a minimum cooling supply humidity ratio of 0.0005, no infiltration, no people, no summary tables, and no HVAC objects at
all. Four time steps per hour move it (01/20 07:00, −145.86 °C). The routine named in the context line, `PsyTwbFnTdbWPb`,
computes the site's outdoor wet-bulb each time step from the weather's dry-bulb, relative humidity, and pressure; at that time
step the outdoor state is 5.6 °C, humidity ratio 0.001519, 100 800 Pa, and EnergyPlus's reported `Site Outdoor Air Wetbulb
Temperature` is −0.4185 °C, equal to an independent ASHRAE calculation (−0.4182 °C). So the iteration passes an out-of-range trial
temperature to the saturation-pressure function, warns, and still converges. Every instance seen is at an outdoor wet-bulb just
below 0 °C (Sterling 03/15 20:00 about −0.43 °C, Tucson 11/17 01:00 about −0.48 °C), where the saturation curve changes from water
to ice; humid climates reach that state with a less dry air and did not warn. Raising the relative humidity of that one record
from 27 % to 28 % (wet-bulb +0.06 °C) removes the warning, and the summed ideal sensible heating and cooling change by at most
1.1 × 10⁻⁴ kWh in any hour and by less than 10⁻³ kWh a year (the humidity change itself). No object of these IDFs uses the outdoor
wet-bulb (ideal loads without outdoor air, no evaporative or plant equipment). **No metric the study reports changes.**

**`FixViewFactors` is inherent to a merged zone that keeps every room's floor and ceiling pieces, with a large internal mass.**
The *Single Zone Per Floor Type* zone over *No Simplification* (`E0`) has 51 surfaces (23 floor pieces, 24 ceiling pieces, 4
walls) and 7 windows; the *Single Zone Per Floor* simplifier's zone of the same floor has 6 surfaces. Both have the same areas
(floor and ceiling 1 660.7 m², exterior walls 659.2 m², closed, the floor equal to the footprint) and the same internal mass,
9 466.7 m², 2.4 times the surface area. EnergyPlus's approximate view factors then miss completeness by 0.005 (it asks for 0.001);
without the internal mass the warning goes, and the 6-surface zone with the same mass does not warn. The geometry is not wrong.
The fragmented zone is still a different EnergyPlus model: its annual sensible cooling differs from the 6-surface zone's by
−0.44 % (*Single Zone Per Floor Type*) and −0.46 % (*Single Zone Building*), heating by +1.0 % and +2.1 % (of 280 kWh), at most
0.6 and 1.3 kWh in an hour, so deduplication rightly keeps both.

## 9. Publication on the atlas site

The owner will publish the zoning recommendation per program, climate zone, and vintage on the atlas website. `report.py` writes
`lod-recommendations.json` (schema `scripts/lod-study/schemas/lod-recommendations-v1.schema.json`), one entry per test case
(prototype × climate zone × vintage × orientation), indexed by atlas program ID (`by_program`):

- the atlas program IDs of each program key, read from the suite's saved inputs;
- the reference's benchmark values: conditioned area, zone count, annual ideal sensible heating and cooling per m² of conditioned
  floor, and their maximum and P99 in kW;
- every test zoning: its §10 metrics as the runner stored them (with their flags), its conditioned-area change, and whether it
  meets the threshold, with each limit it misses; failed and duplicate sites are listed and never recommended;
- the recommended zonings: the coarsest (fewest modelled zones) that meet the threshold, ties all listed.

**The threshold is an owner choice** (default in `report.py`, replaced with `--threshold <file.json>`, recorded in every file):
for each of sensible heating and cooling, |annual error| ≤ 10 %, CV(RMSE) ≤ 30 % and |NMBE| ≤ 10 % (ASHRAE Guideline 14's hourly
calibration limits), and |conditioned-area change| ≤ 1 %; a load whose reference is below 1 kWh/m² a year is not judged. In R1
(3C, where heating is 0.09 kWh/m²) the coarsest zonings meeting it are the four *Perimeter Core* zonings with *Stack* or *Stacked
Floor Zone Multiplier*, 15 zones against the reference's 65. The site integration (how the atlas reads, aggregates over
orientations, and shows the file) is later atlas work.

**The consolidated drop (R6, D-203).** The atlas site's zoning section, https://energy-atlas.github.io/archetype-atlas/zoning/, carries the R3/R4
first drop (both `lod-recommendations.json` files of version 1.0.0). It takes the consolidated drop next, from
`research/lod-study/results/`: `lod-recommendations.json` of schema version 1.1.0 (R3's entries unchanged, plus
the optional `cost` of each timed reference and zoning, and the `timing` block with the gate verdict of D-202, the repeat check, and the medians
by zoning and prototype), `lod-recommendations-ht.json` (R4, separate), and `provenance.json`. A reader of version 1.0.0 that ignores unknown
members reads 1.1.0 unchanged.
